# Verified Research Papers

This collection contains 20 verified scholarly papers relevant to the topic **“Evaluating Multilingual Reliability of Large Language Models in Scientific Literature Analysis.”** The papers are organized thematically across multilingual language models, multilingual evaluation, scientific language models, scientific literature analysis, and reliability evaluation.

---

[Publisher](https://www.cell.com/patterns/fulltext/S2666-3899%2824%2900301-5)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2024.101118)

A comprehensive survey covering multilingual LLM architectures, training, alignment, evaluation, resources, and applications. It provides the broad theoretical background for studying multilingual reliability.

## 2. Multilingual Large Language Models

### 2.1 BLOOM: A 176B-Parameter Open-Access Multilingual Language Model

BigScience Workshop. (2022). *BLOOM: A 176B-Parameter Open-Access Multilingual Language Model*. **arXiv preprint arXiv:2211.05100**.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05100)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.05100)

Introduces BLOOM, a large open multilingual language model developed through the BigScience collaboration. It is an important reference for multilingual foundation-model research.

---

### 2.2 Aya Model: An Instruction Finetuned Open-Access Multilingual Language Model

[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.845)

## 3. Multilingual Evaluation Benchmarks

### 3.1 XTREME-R: Towards More Challenging and Nuanced Multilingual Evaluation

Sebastian Ruder, Noah Constant, Jan Botha, Aditya Siddhant, Orhan Firat, Jinlan Fu, Pengfei Liu, Junjie Hu, Dan Garrette, Graham Neubig, & Melvin Johnson. (2021). *XTREME-R: Towards More Challenging and Nuanced Multilingual Evaluation*. In **Proceedings of the 2021 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)**, 10215–10245.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.802/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2021.emnlp-main.802)

Presents a challenging multilingual evaluation suite spanning 50 languages and multiple natural-language-understanding tasks. It provides a useful framework for investigating cross-lingual performance differences.

---

### 3.2 The FLORES-101 Evaluation Benchmark for Low-Resource and Multilingual Machine Translation

Naman Goyal, Cynthia Gao, Vishrav Chaudhary, Peng-Jen Chen, Guillaume Wenzek, Da Ju, Sanjana Krishnan, Marc’Aurelio Ranzato, Francisco Guzmán, & Angela Fan. (2022). *The FLORES-101 Evaluation Benchmark for Low-Resource and Multilingual Machine Translation*. **Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics, 10**, 522–538.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.30/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00474)

Introduces a professionally translated and fully aligned benchmark covering 101 languages, with particular importance for evaluating low-resource multilingual translation.

---

### 3.3 MEGAVERSE: Benchmarking Large Language Models Across Languages, Modalities, Models and Tasks

Sanchit Ahuja, Divyanshu Aggarwal, Varun Gumma, Ishaan Watts, Ashutosh Sathe, Millicent Ochieng, Rishav Hada, Prachi Jain, Mohamed Ahmed, Kalika Bali, & Sunayana Sitaram. (2024). *MEGAVERSE: Benchmarking Large Language Models Across Languages, Modalities, Models and Tasks*. In **Proceedings of the 2024 Conference of the North American Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics: Human Language Technologies (NAACL-HLT), Volume 1: Long Papers**, 2598–2637.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.143/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.naacl-long.143)

Evaluates large language models across multiple languages, modalities, models, and tasks and provides a broad framework for studying multilingual model performance and reliability.

---

### 3.4 Global MMLU: Understanding and Addressing Cultural and Linguistic Biases in Multilingual Evaluation

[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.919)
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03304)

Investigates linguistic and cultural biases in multilingual benchmark construction and evaluation. It is especially relevant to understanding whether multilingual benchmarks provide fair comparisons across languages.

---

### 3.5 MMLU-ProX: A Multilingual Benchmark for Advanced Large Language Model Evaluation

Weihao Xuan, Rui Yang, Heli Qi, Qingcheng Zeng, Yunze Xiao, Yun Xing, Junjue Wang, Huitao Li, Xin Li, Kunyu Yu, Nan Liu, Qingyu Chen, Douglas Teodoro, Edison Marrese-Taylor, Shijian Lu, Yusuke Iwasawa, Yutaka Matsuo, & Irene Li. ## 4. Scientific Language Models and Literature Analysis

### 4.1 SciBERT: A Pretrained Language Model for Scientific Text

Iz Beltagy, Kyle Lo, & Arman Cohan. (2019). *SciBERT: A Pretrained Language Model for Scientific Text*. In **Proceedings of the 2019 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing and the 9th International Joint Conference on Natural Language Processing (EMNLP-IJCNLP)**, 3615–3620.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1371/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D19-1371)

Introduces SciBERT, a language model pretrained specifically on scientific publications. It provides an important foundation for domain-specific scientific natural language processing.

---

### 4.2 Galactica: A Large Language Model for Science

Ross Taylor, Marcin Kardas, Guillem Cucurull, Thomas Scialom, Anthony Hartshorn, Elvis Saravia, Andrew Poulton, Viktor Kerkez, & Robert Stojnic. (2022). *Galactica: A Large Language Model for Science*. **arXiv preprint arXiv:2211.09085**.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09085)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.09085)

Presents a large language model trained on scientific papers, reference material, knowledge bases, and other scientific resources. The work explores LLMs as interfaces for scientific knowledge and reasoning.

---

### 4.3 PubMedQA: A Dataset for Biomedical Research Question Answering

Qiao Jin, Bhuwan Dhingra, Zhengping Liu, William W. Cohen, & Xinghua Lu. (2019). *PubMedQA: A Dataset for Biomedical Research Question Answering*. In **Proceedings of the 2019 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing and the 9th International Joint Conference on Natural Language Processing (EMNLP-IJCNLP)**, 2567–2577.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1259/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D19-1259)

Introduces a biomedical research question-answering dataset based on PubMed abstracts. It is relevant to evaluating whether language models can reason over scientific evidence.

---

### 4.4 Fact or Fiction: Verifying Scientific Claims

David Wadden, Shanchuan Lin, Kyle Lo, Lucy Lu Wang, Madeleine van Zuylen, Arman Cohan, & Hannaneh Hajishirzi. (2020). *Fact or Fiction: Verifying Scientific Claims*. In **Proceedings of the 2020 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)**, 7534–7550.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.609/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-main.609)

Introduces the SciFact scientific claim-verification task, requiring systems to retrieve evidence from scientific literature and determine whether a claim is supported or refuted.

---

### 4.5 SciReviewGen: A Large-scale Dataset for Automatic Literature Review Generation

Tetsu Kasanishi, Masaru Isonuma, Junichiro Mori, & Ichiro Sakata. (2023). *SciReviewGen: A Large-scale Dataset for Automatic Literature Review Generation*. In **Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2023**, 6695–6715.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.418/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.findings-acl.418)

Introduces a large-scale resource containing literature reviews and cited papers for studying automatic scientific literature-review generation and information synthesis.

---

### 4.6 LitSearch: A Retrieval Benchmark for Scientific Literature Search

Anirudh Ajith, Mengzhou Xia, Alexis Chevalier, Tanya Goyal, Danqi Chen, & Tianyu Gao. (2024). *LitSearch: A Retrieval Benchmark for Scientific Literature Search*. In **Proceedings of the 2024 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)**, 15068–15083.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.840/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.840)

Introduces a benchmark of realistic scientific literature-search questions and evaluates systems for retrieving relevant research papers.

---

### 4.7 PaperQA: Retrieval-Augmented Generative Agent for Scientific Research

Jakub Lála, Odhran O'Donoghue, Aleksandar Shtedritski, Sam Cox, Samuel G. Rodriques, & Andrew D. White. (2023). *PaperQA: Retrieval-Augmented Generative Agent for Scientific Research*. **arXiv preprint arXiv:2312.07559**.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07559)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2312.07559)

Presents a retrieval-augmented approach for answering scientific research questions using information retrieved from scientific papers and providing evidence for generated answers.

## 5. Factuality, Hallucination and Citation Reliability

### 5.1 FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation

Sewon Min, Kalpesh Krishna, Xinxi Lyu, Mike Lewis, Wen-tau Yih, Pang Wei Koh, Mohit Iyyer, Luke Zettlemoyer, & Hannaneh Hajishirzi. (2023). *FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation*. In **Proceedings of the 2023 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)**, 12076–12100.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.741/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.741)

Introduces FActScore, which decomposes generated text into atomic facts and evaluates how many of those facts are supported by reliable evidence.

---

### 5.2 SelfCheckGPT: Zero-Resource Black-Box Hallucination Detection for Generative Large Language Models

Potsawee Manakul, Adian Liusie, & Mark J. F. Gales. (2023). *SelfCheckGPT: Zero-Resource Black-Box Hallucination Detection for Generative Large Language Models*. In **Proceedings of the 2023 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)**, 9004–9017.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.557/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.557)
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08896)

Introduces a black-box hallucination-detection method based on consistency across independently sampled model responses. It is useful for evaluating the factual reliability of generated scientific text.

---

### 5.3 Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations

Tianyu Gao, Howard Yen, Jiatong Yu, & Danqi Chen. (2023). *Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations*. In **Proceedings of the 2023 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)**, 6465–6488.

[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.398)

Introduces ALCE, a benchmark for evaluating LLM-generated answers with citations. It evaluates dimensions including fluency, correctness, and citation quality and is directly relevant to evidence-grounded scientific literature analysis.
